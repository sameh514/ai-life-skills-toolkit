NARRATIVE SPINE
[Person/system] wants to [concrete goal]. The obstacle is [problem]. We follow
the attempt from [starting scene] through [turning point] to [useful resolution].

TONE AND PACING
- Calm, curious narrative journalism with a strong story thread.
- Short spoken sentences; one idea per sentence.
- Human-scale examples before formal vocabulary.
- Do not imitate any named show, host, script, or distinctive phrasing.
- Avoid lecture openings, textbook definitions, and documentation language.

JARGON BUDGET
- Introduce at most one unfamiliar technical term at a time.
- First explain the idea without the term.
- Then name and define it in one short sentence.
- Reuse one stable plain-English translation.
- Remove optional acronyms and specialist wording.

FORMAT AND LENGTH
- [single narrator / two-host discussion / deep dive]
- NotebookLM length: Default unless the user chooses another setting.
- [number] story chapters, each centered on one question.

STORY CHAPTERS
1. Cold open: [specific action, mystery, or failure]
2. What the listener needs to notice: [plain-language idea]
3. Reveal the mechanism: [formal concept after intuition]
4. Complication: [misconception or failure]
5. Resolution: [what now makes sense]
6. Why it matters for the course task: [paper/IDE handoff]

ACTIVE RECALL
- Ask one short question at each designated checkpoint.
- Say that “I do not know yet” counts as a committed answer.
- Leave 3–5 seconds of real quiet; this duration is a practical hypothesis, and
  the listener may pause the player for longer.
- Answer plainly, explain why, then contrast one tempting misconception.
- Explicitly resolve every opening question or invited guess.
- From episode two onward, ask one prior-episode free-recall question before
  giving any recap.
- Do not turn every transition into a quiz.

MUST COVER
- [concept and concrete example]
- [concept and concrete example]
- [course-specific action or trace]

PRONUNCIATION
- [term] = [spoken form]
- Speak code by meaning; do not read underscores or punctuation literally.

DO NOT
- Do not begin with a glossary or learning-objective list.
- Do not stack acronyms or definitions.
- Do not use unexplained specialist wording.
- Do not claim unsupported course facts.
- Do not reveal answer keys or graded-assignment solutions.
- Do not add generic filler or unrelated history.

CHECK THE FINISHED EPISODE
- [platform transcript path or timestamped manual-review record]
- [recall checkpoints present and answered]
- [invited questions resolved]
- [spoken-density and jargon result]
- [source-claim check]
- [assessment leakage check]
